I am a PhD student at ETH Zurich, where I am fortunate to be advised by Prof. Afonso Bandeira. Currently I am visiting the Simons Institute in Berkeley.

My research is motivated by three fundamental questions about randomness:

  • How can complex phenomena be modelled by random objects?
  • How do procedures and algorithms perform on typical, or random, inputs?
  • How can randomness be leveraged to improve the performance of algorithms?

Such questions often arise in statistics, computer science, optimization, data science, and numerical linear algebra, and are often answered by random matrix theory, which has been the common thread of my research so far.

research

  1. The Spectral Distribution of Matrix Least Squares Solutions, and the Ellipsoid Fitting Problem
    with Afonso S. Bandeira and Yue M. Lu
    Available online, 2026
    Abs arXiv

    We study the spectral distribution of the least squares solution to a random linear system whose unknown is a symmetric matrix. In the normalized and isotropic setting, we derive a closed-form description of the support of the spectral distribution. As an application, we consider the ellipsoid fitting problem: given n standard Gaussian vectors in ℝ^d, does there exist a centered ellipsoid passing through all of them? It was recently shown that this problem has a sharp phase transition at n ≈d^2/4. On the satisfiability side, unlike earlier results, these sharp results do not rely on least squares candidates. We show that the least squares candidates considered in earlier works have a threshold of positive semi-definiteness at n ≈d^2/10, which was previously observed numerically and predicted non-rigorously using statistical physics methods. Moreover, we show that the corresponding least squares dual certificates have a threshold of positive definiteness at n ≈2d^2/5. Our approach combines several matrix concentration tools. A key technical ingredient, which may be of independent interest, is showing that a certain kernel matrix is well-conditioned with high probability until n ≈d^2/2.

  2. Convergence of Kikuchi matrices to Γ-independent and q-Gaussian limits
    with Afonso S. Bandeira, Dmitriy Kunisky, Lucas Pesenti, and Robert Wang
    Available online, 2026
    Abs arXiv

    Kikuchi matrices are a family of structured matrices that were introduced to study problems involving tensors and hypergraphs. We show that, as the ambient dimension grows, dense random Kikuchi matrices have a limit described by a system of Γ-independent semicircular elements. This characterizes their limiting spectral distribution and yields improved bounds on their spectral norm, a key quantity in the analysis of algorithms for Tensor PCA. Finally, we show that, in an appropriate double limit, independent Kikuchi matrices converge to the q-Gaussian system, another central object in noncommutative probability.

  3. Matrix Concentration and Equivalent Operators on Fock Spaces
    with Afonso S. Bandeira, Dmitriy Kunisky, Lucas Pesenti, and Robert Wang
    Available online, 2026
    Abs arXiv Video

    We develop a method for proving matrix concentration inequalities by identifying random matrices with associated deterministic operators acting on suitable Fock spaces and controlling norms of these operators and their restrictions to low-order subspaces. Applying this method, we obtain new strengthenings of the non-commutative Khintchine inequality that improve on the state of the art, in particular sharpening recent inequalities due to Bandeira, Boedihardjo, and van Handel (2023) quantifying intrinsic freeness of random matrices. Our proofs of these results are based on relatively simple operator algebra arguments and involve neither Gaussian interpolation nor explicit combinatorics of tracial moments. Further, our techniques apply equally well to several models of non-commutative random variables of interest in the literature, such as operator series constructed from q-Gaussian and Γ-independent systems of operators, treating all of these objects with the same method. We obtain new norm bounds for such operators both in the style of the non-commutative Khintchine inequality and in the style of Lehner’s operator norm formula.

  4. The Hypergraph Moore Bound
    with Afonso S. Bandeira, Dmitriy Kunisky, Lucas Pesenti, and Robert Wang
    Available online, 2026
    Abs arXiv

    We prove Feige’s conjectured hypergraph Moore bound, which controls the size of the smallest even cover in a k-uniform hypergraph in terms of the average density of hyperedges. We give a simple proof of Feige’s original conjecture for all k ≥3, with no superfluous polylogarithmic factors. Our method uses colored walks in Kikuchi graphs together with polynomial methods in the even-uniform case, and extends to odd-uniform hypergraphs via an adapted procedure.

  5. Matrix Chaos Inequalities and Chaos of Combinatorial Type
    with Afonso S. Bandeira, Kevin Lucca, and Ramon van Handel
    In Proceedings of the 57th Annual ACM Symposium on Theory of Computing, Prague, Czechia, 2025
    Abs DOI arXiv Video

    Matrix concentration inequalities and their recently discovered sharp counterparts provide powerful tools to bound the spectrum of random matrices whose entries are linear functions of independent random variables. However, in many applications in theoretical computer science and in other areas one encounters more general random matrix models, called matrix chaoses, whose entries are polynomials of independent random variables. Such models have often been studied on a case-by-case basis using ad-hoc methods that can yield suboptimal dimensional factors. In this paper we provide general matrix concentration inequalities for matrix chaoses, which enable the treatment of such models in a systematic manner. These inequalities are expressed in terms of flattenings of the coefficients of the matrix chaos. We further identify a special family of matrix chaoses of combinatorial type for which the flattening parameters can be computed mechanically by a simple rule. This allows us to provide a unified treatment of and improved bounds for matrix chaoses that arise in a variety of applications, including graph matrices, Khatri-Rao matrices, and matrices that arise in average case analysis of the sum-of-squares hierarchy.

  6. The bunkbed conjecture holds in the p↑1 limit
    with Tom Hutchcroft and Alexander Kent
    Combinatorics, Probability and Computing, 2023
    Abs DOI arXiv

    Let G=(V,E) be a countable graph. The Bunkbed graph of G is the product graph G \times K_2, which has vertex set V\times {0,1} with “horizontal” edges inherited from G and additional “vertical” edges connecting (w,0) and (w,1) for each w ∈V. Kasteleyn’s Bunkbed conjecture states that for each u,v ∈V and p∈[0,1], the vertex (u,0) is at least as likely to be connected to (v,0) as to (v,1) under Bernoulli-p bond percolation on the bunkbed graph. We prove that the conjecture holds in the p ↑1 limit in the sense that for each finite graph G there exists \varepsilon (G)\gt 0 such that the bunkbed conjecture holds for p \geqslant 1-\varepsilon (G).
